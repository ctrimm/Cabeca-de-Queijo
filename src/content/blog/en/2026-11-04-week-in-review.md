It was not a clean win, and it was not a comfortable one. But it was a win, and on a short week with a third-string center and a linebacker corps held together by backups, that counts for plenty. Green Bay beat Carolina **27-19** on Thursday night at Lambeau, survived three turnovers and a late Panthers push, and heads to New England at **5-3**.

---

## 27-19: A Win That Was Never Quite Comfortable

The scoring tells the shape of the night. **Jordan Love** threw a **12-yard touchdown to Tucker Kraft** in the first quarter — the extra point failed, so it was only 6-3 — and after Chuba Hubbard put Carolina up 10-6 early in the second, Green Bay answered twice before halftime: **Josh Jacobs on a 1-yard run** and **Matthew Golden on a 24-yard catch**, the second set up by the defensive play of the night (below). It was **20-10 at the half**, and **27-10 in the third** after Love hit Jacobs on a **4-yard touchdown pass**.

Then the Packers stopped scoring, and Carolina kept coming. **Jalen Coker's 26-yard touchdown with 5:37 left** made it **27-19** — the two-point try failed — and the final minutes were a hold-your-breath exercise before Carolina's last chance died on downs and a final fumble sealed it.

Love finished **30-of-41 for 288 yards, three touchdowns and one interception**, a **106.6 rating**, and he was not sacked once. **Matthew Golden** caught **7 passes for 79 yards and a touchdown**, and **Tucker Kraft** caught **all 6 of his targets for 53 yards and a score**. Green Bay was **3-for-3 in the red zone** — the one area of the offense that worked exactly as drawn up.

The mess: three turnovers, including a fumble on the opening kickoff that handed Carolina a short field, and Love's fourth-quarter interception near the goal line that took a potential game-sealing field goal off the board. Love said afterward that pick would "haunt" him. Fair enough — and also the kind of honesty that makes the rest of the night easier to believe.

---

## Watson: The Story of the Night

Forget the box score for a moment, even though it is a good one: **8 catches for 113 yards**.

Midway through the second quarter, **Christian Watson** hauled in a **53-yard deep ball** from Love, landed on the football, and could not breathe. He was coughing up blood on the sideline, and the medical staff sent him to St. Vincent Hospital by ambulance for precautionary testing — a CT scan and bloodwork. Both came back clear, apart from the injury itself: **a broken rib**.

And then he came back. Watson returned to Lambeau in the same ambulance, ran out of the tunnel midway through the third quarter to a huge ovation, extra rib padding molded around his midsection, and finished the game. His final catch — a **7-yard grab on third-and-2 with 2:33 left** — forced Carolina to burn its last timeout and let Green Bay bleed most of the clock.

Watson said afterward he never wanted to go to the hospital, and that he has no plans to miss snaps: *"We'll be good to go next week."* It was, by any measure, the gutsiest performance of the season so far — his third 100-yard game of the year.

---

## Watson for New England: Read This Carefully

This is the part where we refuse to get ahead of the news.

As of Wednesday morning, Watson is **listed as Questionable with a rib fracture** in the aggregators' Week 9 listings (updated overnight Nov. 4), and that listing is **not an official designation** — it is a pre-report placeholder. On Monday, passing-game coordinator **Jason Vrable** said Watson is sore but **"doing great"** and that the team expects him to play through it at New England, adding **"we'll see as the week goes."** Watson himself has said he expects to play.

But the first official Week 9 practice report is due Wednesday, likely after this post publishes, and the final game designation is not due until Friday. So the honest version is this: **listed Questionable, expected to suit up per the team on Monday, official status still to be confirmed. Do not treat him as cleared yet.** We will update this space if the Wednesday or Friday report changes the picture.

## Hopper's Breakout, and a Defense That Carried the Night

With **Edgerrin Cooper out for the season (Achilles)** and **Zaire Franklin week-to-week (shin)**, Green Bay started its No. 3 and No. 4 linebackers. The No. 4 stole the show.

**Ty'Ron Hopper** — **8 tackles, a sack, an interception, and 3 pass breakups** — tipped a Bryce Young pass to himself late in the second quarter, and that takeaway set up Golden's touchdown and the 20-10 halftime lead. Matt LaFleur, unprompted: *"I can't say enough great things about him and the night he had."* Per SI's snap-count analysis, Hopper became the team's first linebacker since 2000 with a sack, an interception, and at least three passes defensed in a game. **Isaiah McDuffie** added **10 tackles** alongside him.

The unit as a whole sacked Young **6 times, from six different players**, pressured him on nearly half of his dropbacks, and held Carolina to **3.7 yards per play** and **256 total yards**. Young finished **23-of-41 for 209 yards, one touchdown and one interception**. This defense has now won Green Bay more than one game this season. Thursday was the clearest case yet.

The other side of that coin: the running game is still stuck. Green Bay rushed for just **66 yards** as a team, and Jacobs — two touchdowns and all — managed only **39 yards on 21 carries (1.9 per carry)**, both scores coming on third down inside the 5. Both of Jacobs' touchdowns mattered enormously. Neither changes the fact that this offense cannot currently run the ball, and New England will have noticed.

---

## Parsons: The Timeline Firms Up

Monday brought the next step on **Micah Parsons**. LaFleur said it is the team's **"intention"** for Parsons to return to practice this week — individual drills first, team drills next week — and multiple reports continue to point at the **Thanksgiving Eve game at the Rams on Nov. 25** as the target for his first game action since tearing his ACL on Dec. 14, 2025.

He is **not** playing at New England, and Nov. 25 is a target, not a promise — the 21-day practice window, once opened, is a ramp, not a debut date. But the direction of travel is now unmistakable, and it is toward Green Bay.

---

## Next Up: At New England, Sunday

**Sunday, Nov. 8 — 4:25 p.m. ET / 3:25 p.m. CT / 6:25 p.m. Brasília time, at the New England Patriots, Gillette Stadium, Foxborough, on FOX.**

Both teams are **5-3**. New England is **3-0 at home** after beating Miami **24-19** on Nov. 1, with **Drake Maye** going **16-of-20 for 199 yards, two touchdowns and a rushing score** — **11 touchdowns to 1 interception over his last four games**. Green Bay is **2-2 on the road**. New England opened as a **2.5-point favorite (FanDuel; over/under 47.5)**, and **Tom Brady** calls the game for FOX's lead crew — his first Patriots game as a broadcaster. The last meeting: Packers **27**, Patriots **24** in overtime at Lambeau in 2022.

The stakes, per The Athletic's simulator: Green Bay's playoff odds sit at **47%** now — **61% with a win, 39% with a loss**. That is what Sunday is worth. The early Foxborough forecast is one to check again before kickoff: models disagree, with highs in the upper 50s cooling into the low 50s after the **4:30 p.m. sunset** and a rain chance anywhere from low to around 50% depending on the model.

After this one: **the Vikings at Lambeau on Nov. 15**, then the **Week 11 bye**.

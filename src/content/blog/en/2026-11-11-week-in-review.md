The best offensive afternoon of the season, on the road, in a building where no visitor had ever done what the Packers just did. And still, somehow, this team cannot get through a Sunday without the injury report eating a starter. Let's get through it.

---

## 45-27: The Avalanche

The tone was set on the second play of the game. **Drake Maye's first pass** was intercepted by **Xavier McKinney**, who returned it **36 yards to the New England 14**. Two plays later, **Jordan Love** scrambled **13 yards for a touchdown** — Green Bay led 101 seconds in, and essentially never looked back.

It was **14-0 after one quarter** and **17-0 early in the second**. New England answered — a **30-yard wheel-route touchdown to Rhamondre Stevenson**, another score before the break — and it was **24-14 at halftime**. Then the third quarter removed all doubt: **18 unanswered points**, a **42-14 lead**, and a fourth quarter that was mostly paperwork. A **Trey Smack field goal** made it 45-21, a late Patriots touchdown and a failed two-point try set the final margin, and both teams finished with their backup quarterbacks.

The totals: **478 yards of offense**, a season high. **Five touchdowns and three field goals.** And the headline number: **45 points is the most a visiting team has ever scored at Gillette Stadium.** In more than 25 years in that building, nobody had hung more than 42 on the Patriots there. The Packers had 42 with four minutes left in the third quarter.

Green Bay is **6-3**, winners of **five of its last six**.

---

## Watson, on a Broken Rib

Ten days ago in this space, the Christian Watson story was a 53-yard catch, an ambulance ride, and a coughing-up-blood quote that turned out to be a **broken rib**. The story is different now.

Watson played Sunday — on the rib — and produced the best game of his career: **seven catches, 171 yards and a touchdown**. And he saved it for late. At halftime he had three catches for 62 yards; the second half was the show. The touchdown came after the break, against a secondary built around All-Pro **Christian Gonzalez**, and Watson spent the afternoon running past it.

Next to him, **Matthew Golden** had a career day of his own, front-loaded instead of back-loaded: **10 catches, 164 yards and a touchdown**, with seven catches and 150 of those yards by halftime. Combined line for the pair: **17 catches on 18 targets, 335 yards, two touchdowns**. There is not a better one-two receiving day in the league this season.

Love's final line almost reads like a misprint: **23-of-29 for 372 yards, three touchdowns, no interceptions, a 153.2 passer rating** — plus the rushing touchdown that opened the scoring.

One footnote, because it's fun and because it is apparently now a recurring feature: Watson and Golden celebrated with a **double backflip**, and Matt LaFleur has reportedly asked them to stop. Given what the training room looks like below, it's hard to blame him.

---

## Four Takeaways

The defense started the avalanche and kept it rolling. **Four takeaways**, converted into **three touchdowns**.

McKinney's two interceptions — the first on Maye's opening throw, the second late in the first half — give him **five on the season**. **Evan Williams** got the third pick of the half, on a deep ball tipped by **Brandon Cisse**, October's Defensive Rookie of the Month; Williams, the NFC's Defensive Player of the Month for October, now has three interceptions of his own. **Isaiah McDuffie** stripped Stevenson and recovered the fumble himself. Behind them, **four sacks**, two by **Karl Brooks**.

Maye's line tells the story from the other side: **21-of-38 for 299 yards, three touchdowns and three interceptions** — all three picks in the first half, all three, per the live coverage, thrown from clean pockets. Maye now leads the NFL with **11 interceptions** in nine games. New England is 5-4, and its quarterback owned the loss afterward. Fair enough. This one was his — and Green Bay's defense made sure of it.

---

## Smack, Quietly Perfect

Two weeks after the Detroit game, where this space gave the rookie kicker his own section for the wrong reasons: **Smack was perfect on Sunday. Three field goals in three tries**, including a **38-yarder** in the fourth quarter, plus his extra points. No drama, no story. That's exactly what you want from the position, and it's worth saying out loud after the month he's had.

---

## Jacobs: Surgery, and a Backfield by Committee

Now the bill. On the first series, **Josh Jacobs** stiff-armed Patriots defensive back Craig Woodson, his hand caught in Woodson's facemask, and that was his afternoon: **four carries, five yards**, two trips to the locker room, done. By his own account afterward, he popped the finger back into place himself on the field.

On Monday, LaFleur confirmed the diagnosis: a **fractured ring finger on Jacobs' right (ball-carrying) hand**, and **surgery this week** to repair it. The hope Sunday night that he might play through it with protection died quickly — LaFleur said Jacobs tried to return and **"just had no grip strength."** Playing through it, per the head coach, was never really an option.

How long is he out? The honest answer is that nobody has said, and the two reported versions differ:

- **CBS Sports' Matt Zenitz** reports Jacobs is expected to miss **a maximum of three weeks** — a timeline that would keep him off injured reserve entirely and have him back before the end of the month.
- **LaFleur would not rule out injured reserve.** IR counts games, not weeks, and the Packers' bye sits inside the window: an IR stint would cost Jacobs the Vikings, Rams, Saints and Bills games, making the **Dec. 20 home game against Miami** his earliest possible return.

Either way, he is out for Sunday. The backfield now belongs to **Kaleb Johnson** — who stepped in at Foxborough and led the team with **14 carries for 68 yards and his first career touchdown** — with **MarShawn Lloyd** and **Pierre Strong** behind him. Lloyd himself was inactive at New England with a knee injury, so the other name to watch is **Chris Brooks**, who is eligible to come off injured reserve this week — LaFleur pointed to exactly that on Monday when asked why the team could absorb the loss. One caveat LaFleur raised himself: there are pass-protection concerns with Johnson that the coaches will have to scheme around. Minnesota's defense will have read that quote too.

This is game four of Jacobs' season. He missed the first six on the commissioner's exempt list, returned for three — **148 scrimmage yards and three touchdowns** — and is gone again. The Packers built this offense expecting him to be its spine. So far, the spine has been available for about a month, total.

## The Injury Sweep: The Linebacker Room, Mostly

Same rule as every week — everything we found, not just the headline.

**The linebacker room is the emergency.** **Ty'Ron Hopper**, starting in place of Zaire Franklin, was ruled out early with a **chest injury** — per LaFleur, an injury that actually happened during **pregame warmups**. Hopper managed two snaps; **Nick Niemann** finished the game. The early word is that it **does not sound like significant missed time**, which the Packers badly need to be true, because late in the game **Isaiah McDuffie was shaken up too**, leaving sixth-option **Kristian Welch** on the field. **Zaire Franklin** (hairline fracture in his shin) was inactive for a second straight week; LaFleur has said he will not go on injured reserve, and his status for Sunday is genuinely unknown. If Franklin, Hopper and McDuffie are all limited this week, Green Bay is down to the practice squad at the position. Watch the Wednesday and Thursday practice reports.

**Bo Melton** was **evaluated for a concussion** and is in the protocol process; no clearance news yet. Slot corner **Javon Bullard** came in and out of the game with a foot or leg issue. **Jordan Love** needed tape on a finger after a first-half sack and played on without visible trouble — his line is above. **Evan Williams** went down in the second half and returned.

**Sean Rhyan** (high-ankle sprain) remains out and, per Zenitz, is expected to miss **at least a month** — the makeshift interior line held up again Sunday, which remains one of the quiet stories of this season. Tight end **Josh Whyle** (concussion) and tackle **Travis Glover** (knee) were both inactive at New England.

And the season-ending list is unchanged: Jayden Reed, Zach Bako-Bewele, Edgerrin Cooper and Javon Hargrave.

---

## Parsons: The Clock Is Running

The update here is short because the Week 8 post covered the news and Sunday changed nothing: **Micah Parsons' 21-day practice window opened on Wednesday, Nov. 4** — his first practice in **324 days**, individual drills only — and he was ruled out for New England on Friday, as expected. He is **not expected to play against Minnesota** either.

Day 21 of the window is **Nov. 25, the Thanksgiving Eve game at the Rams**, and that remains the target everyone is pointing at without quite promising. Parsons himself has been blunt about the ramp: the injury was an **ACL reconstruction plus a full meniscus repair**, he spent seven to eight weeks on crutches, his conditioning is, in his words, **"probably the worst it's been in my career,"** and when he does debut it will be in a situational pass-rush role, not 50 or 60 snaps. November flashes, December build, full version later. Treat the Rams date as a target, not a promise — but the clock is now visibly running.

---

## Next Up: The Rematch

**Sunday, Nov. 15 — 1:00 p.m. ET / 12:00 p.m. CT / 15:00 Brasília, vs. the Minnesota Vikings at Lambeau Field, on FOX.**

This is the Week 1 rematch: **Minnesota won the opener 39-22**, and Green Bay has been chasing that loss in the standings ever since. The Vikings arrive at **6-2** but wobbling — **two straight losses**, at Detroit in Week 8 and a **23-6 Monday night loss to Buffalo** to close Week 9, in a game where their offense never got started.

The division, meanwhile, is absurd. After Week 9: **Chicago 7-2, Detroit 6-2, Minnesota 6-2, Green Bay 6-3** — a combined **25-9**, with all four teams sitting in playoff position today. The Bears are on their bye this week, so the top of the table is there to be pulled closer. Win Sunday and the Packers flip the Week 1 result, pull even with Minnesota in the loss column, and head into their own bye with the season bending the right way. Lose it, and they're two back with the Rams, Saints and Bills waiting on the other side of the bye.

No Jacobs, a linebacker room held together with tape, and the division leaders at the door. It was never going to be simple.

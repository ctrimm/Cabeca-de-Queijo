The bye week was supposed to be the quiet one. It was not. Green Bay played nobody and still had the busiest week of its season: the most expensive defensive player in franchise history got cleared to play, the starting running back got ruled all but out, and all three teams ahead of the Packers in the NFC North played — and two of them won. The Packers are 6-4, still last in the division, and now they walk into the first Thanksgiving Eve game in NFL history, on the road, against a team that has won four straight. Let's get through it.

---

## Parsons: Cleared

This is the news the whole bye week was building toward, and on Monday it finally landed: **Micah Parsons received final medical clearance to play**, Ian Rapoport reported, after speaking with Dr. Neal ElAttrache — the surgeon who repaired his knee — earlier in the day. Barring a setback, Parsons makes his Packers debut on **Wednesday night against the Rams**, his first appearance of the 2026 season. The Packers **activated him to the 53-man roster on Tuesday**, and he is officially listed as **questionable** — the formality, not a doubt, after three limited practices.

The numbers behind the wait are worth sitting with for a second. Parsons tore his **ACL, with meniscus damage, at Denver on Dec. 14** — **49 weeks** before Wednesday night. The Packers opened his **21-day practice window on Nov. 4**, and that window closes on **Nov. 25, the game itself**: the team has to activate him by then or he cannot play at all this season. The Packers **waived running back Pierre Strong Jr. late last week** to open the roster spot for him. He took his **first padded practice on Sunday**, was a **limited participant on Monday**, and Matt LaFleur — who has played this as close to the vest as anything all year — would only say after Sunday's session that Parsons "did well," adding before Monday's practice that there was still another day of practice to go: "we'll see."

So keep the hedge where it belongs: clearance is real, and so is the fact that nobody has promised a snap count. What anyone is actually promising for Wednesday is a **situational pass-rush version** of Parsons, ramping up over the coming weeks — not a full-time player on day one. The Rams, for their part, are not pretending otherwise: offensive coordinator Nate Scheelhaase told reporters on Sunday, "we're expecting him to play."

November flashes, December build — that was the plan when the window opened, and the plan is now on the field. Or at least on the edge of it, in a jersey, on Wednesday night.

---

## Jacobs: Unlikely

The other half of the bye-week injury story moved the other way. **Josh Jacobs**, who had surgery on the **broken ring finger on his right hand** and missed the Minnesota game, practiced on a limited basis on Sunday and Monday — but Matt LaFleur said Monday that Jacobs is **not expected to play Wednesday**, and the reporting since has been consistent: he is practicing this week so he can stay sharp for a **potential return next week**.

That is a real comedown from last Tuesday, when Rapoport reported Jacobs had a "realistic chance" to return for this very game. The timeline was always the shorter end of a two-to-three-week window, and the shorter end is not cooperating. No injured-reserve stint was ever needed, and none is now — this is a matter of a surgically repaired hand being able to grip and protect a football, and it is not there yet.

Without him, the Packers roll with the same committee for a second straight week: **Kaleb Johnson**, who took the first running-back reps in the open portion of Sunday's practice, **Chris Brooks**, and **MarShawn Lloyd**. It worked well enough against New England two weeks ago. Against a Rams defense allowing **18.7 points per game**, it is a much harder ask — and it leaves Love, again, without the release valve that keeps defenses honest.

---

## The Division Moved On

Green Bay rested. The rest of the North did not.

**Detroit beat Tampa Bay 31-21** on Sunday for its **sixth straight win**: Jahmyr Gibbs ran for two touchdowns on 98 yards, Jared Goff threw for 279 and two scores, including a 57-yarder to Jameson Williams. **Chicago beat New Orleans 21-13** for its **fifth straight**: Caleb Williams threw two touchdowns, and Jeffery Simmons had three of the Bears' six sacks. And **Minnesota lost 35-10 to San Francisco in Mexico City** — Brock Purdy threw for 255 yards and three touchdowns, Kirk Cousins threw for 235 and was intercepted once, and the Vikings' only touchdown came from Aaron Jones.

So the standings after Week 11: **Detroit 8-2, Chicago 8-2, Minnesota 7-3, Green Bay 6-4**. The Packers are two games back of the top with seven to play, **1-3 inside the division**, and the two teams ahead of them play each other on Thanksgiving Day — Detroit hosts Chicago, and the winner goes to 9-2 while Green Bay is trying to get to 7-4 on Wednesday night. There is no more margin. There has not been for weeks, but now the arithmetic says so out loud.

---

## The Injury Sweep: A Perfect Practice Week, Honestly Earned

The one unambiguously good thing to come out of the bye: for the **third consecutive day, all 52 players on the roster participated in practice**. After two months of a roster held together with tape, the mini-bye did what it was supposed to do.

The final injury report is out: eight Packers are officially **questionable** — Parsons (knee), Jacobs (finger), Zaire Franklin (shin), Travis Glover (shoulder, added Monday), Bo Melton (thigh), Skyy Moore (ankle), Sean Rhyan (ankle) and Kevin Zeitler (triceps). Two names came off the limited list on Tuesday: cornerback Brandon Cisse (rib) and linebacker Isaiah McDuffie (rib) were full participants and carry no game designation, along with guard Aaron Banks (knee). Limited is not out, and nobody new was added to the season-ending column — but the inactives, 90 minutes before kickoff Wednesday, are still the final word on Parsons' snap count and Jacobs.

---

## Next Up: The Rams, on a Wednesday

**Wednesday, Nov. 25 — at the Los Angeles Rams, SoFi Stadium, 8:00 PM ET on Netflix.** The NFL's **first-ever Thanksgiving Eve game**, and just the second Wednesday regular-season game in Packers franchise history (the other was in 1938, against the Chicago Cardinals, in Buffalo).

Both teams are **6-4**, both are coming off the bye, and the winner takes the head-to-head tiebreaker that could decide the final NFC playoff seed. The Rams have won **four straight** despite major injuries on offense — **Puka Nacua is on injured reserve** (MCL surgery, out for the season) and **Davante Adams is on injured reserve too** (ruptured hamstring tendon; a mid-December return is the likely timeline, Week 13 a long shot), so Matthew Stafford is without his top two receivers. Tight end Terrance Ferguson is out for the year, and starting linemen Steve Avila and Coleman Shelton are ruled out; the Rams are getting defensive lineman **Kobie Turner** back instead. The Rams are **2.5-point favorites**; the total is **44**.

History is on Green Bay's side, for whatever that is worth on a Wednesday night in Inglewood: the Packers have won **10 of the last 11 against the Rams**, including each of the last five, and won **24-19 at SoFi in 2024**. The all-time regular-season series is **49-46-2** in Green Bay's favor. After that comes the second of two straight road games — **Week 13 at the Saints** — and then the Bills. There is no soft landing in this stretch, and there never was.

A week to heal, a debut 49 weeks in the making, and a game that could decide whether the division race is still a race. It was never going to be simple.
